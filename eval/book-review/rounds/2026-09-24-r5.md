## What the book and the cheatsheet were teaching

Two claims in `book/en/16_equality.vibe.md`, repeated in the Japanese
chapter and in `book/en/20_pitfalls.vibe.md`:

1. A generic struct has one comparator. `Box[Double]`, `Box[Bytes]`, and
   any array or struct argument compare by address. Annotation does not
   help. The book called this a known defect still being fixed.
2. Pushing a name or a call result into an unannotated `let xs = []`,
   then comparing once both sides are non-empty, fails at run time.

`fixtures/generic_derive_eq_test.vibe` expects
`Box[Double]::{ value: 1.5 } == Box[Double]::{ value: 1.5 }` to be
`true`. `fixtures/eq_bound_derive_test.vibe` expects the same shape
through `[T: Eq]`, including `Double`. The cheatsheet's own #2195
paragraph already said those instantiations are structural. The book
had not caught up with that paragraph, and it contradicted it.

The run-time-trap claim contradicts the compiler. `eq_refusals_report`
in `lib/@vibe/compiler/normalize/desugar_trait_dict.vibe` turns an
unanswerable structural comparison into a build error. On the lane
`vibe run` / `vibe test` use, a pushed name whose element is on the
allow-list is answered by content (#2391, written up in the cheatsheet
just above the sentence that still said "fails at run time").

A third claim, in the cheatsheet rather than the book: "#2523, open
P0", builtin `Eq` is a marker, and `[T: Eq]` on a `derive (Eq)` struct
compares by reference identity. `builtin_traits.vibe` does still spell
`export trait Eq` with no methods. The checker treats that trait as
method-bearing and injects `equals`. The fixture above is the
measurement. `docs/spec/0-1.md` already said this. The cheatsheet
section a reader would find first did not.

The lambda-bound pitfall ("a bound on a lambda is not honoured",
#2737) was the same kind of leftover. Nested bounds have been their own
witnesses since #2778 (`fixtures/lambda_bound_nested_witness_test.vibe`).
The section also said `==` under such a bound falls back to reference
identity, which is the pre-#2523 story.

`lib/@vibe/compiler/tests/marker_cmp_bound_test.vibe` still describes
`Eq` as a marker in its header. Its assertions are about a program that
declares its own methodless `trait Eq`, which is still refused. The
header is easy to misread as a claim about the builtin. Left for a
later pass. Not scored.
